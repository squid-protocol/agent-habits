# Size and shape by model (EXPLORATORY, not pre-registered)

1. **Codex writes the smallest programs, and crams them onto very few lines.** Counted in characters (non-whitespace, so line packing doesn't matter), its program was smaller than Sonnet's in 9 of 10 cells and smaller than Haiku's in 10 of 10. Round 2 shows the same: S2 9/10, K2 9/10. It also put more characters on each line than Sonnet in 10 of 10 cells (median 51 vs 20 per line; S2 also 10/10). In 3 runs a whole chess engine sits on 34-55 lines. So line counts overstate the gap: medians are 110 lines vs Sonnet's 532, but 8.3k vs 13.2k characters.
2. **Haiku writes the most code but keeps each function simpler.** It wrote more characters than Sonnet in 10/10 cells (round 2: 9/10) and more functions in 9/10 (round 2: 9/10). Yet its most tangled function had fewer decision points than Sonnet's in 10/10 cells, and round 2 repeated that 10/10 (median 30 vs 68).
3. **Scale check: each model repeats itself far more closely than it matches another model.** In the same cell, Sonnet vs Sonnet and Haiku vs Haiku programs differ by a median 7% in characters, against 53% between models (ratio 0.13). For function count it is 13% vs 58%, and for the largest function's decision points 19% vs 35%.

Exploratory sign tests (two-sided, exact, ties dropped): Codex smaller than Sonnet 9/1, p = 0.021. Codex denser than Sonnet 10/0, p = 0.002. Haiku's top function simpler than Sonnet's 10/0, p = 0.002.

Caveats: this is an exploratory follow-up on only 10 cells, with about 60 cell counts made (10 measures x 6 model pairs), so treat any single count as a hypothesis. Round-2 checks against Codex reuse the same Codex runs. Per-arm medians in summary.json pool all languages and both tasks. Function measures leave out chess-c-codex-r1, chess-rust-codex-r1 and chess-rust-codex-r2, where the scanner found no functions even in the D16 rescan; their size comes from the source (non-blank lines). For Codex's packed code, line-based function length means little (median 1 line in chess-java and sat-c). sat-c-codex-r1 (D16 rescan) is the one program that failed the check. Test files in the cell's language are counted. One result did not repeat: Haiku had a smaller share of functions with more than 10 decision points than Sonnet in 10/10 cells, but only 3/10 in round 2.
