**Plain reading (exploratory follow-up, not registered).** Three habits survived both the move from human steering to autonomous runs and the move to newer models: Codex leaves functions early more often (Codex higher in 9 of 10 steered pairs, 9 of 10 cells against Sonnet, 8 of 10 against Haiku), Codex writes more complex functions, and Claude uses more bitwise operations (each 5 of 5 steered pairs, then 5 or 6 of 7 cells against each Claude model). Heavy commenting survived only as a Haiku habit: Haiku documents a median 35 % of its functions (10 of 10 cells ahead of Codex), the same rate as steered Claude, while Sonnet documents a median 0 % and writes about a tenth of steered Claude's comments (0.011 vs 0.114 comment lines per code line), though it still out-comments Codex in 8 of 10 cells. Python helper files, Claude's higher fan-out and `for` use, and most rare keyword leans did not survive (fan-out reversed: Codex higher in 7 of 7 cells against Sonnet, 5 of 7 against Haiku), and because steering, model generation and program size all changed at once, none of these losses can be blamed on steering alone.
