# Wiring by model (EXPLORATORY follow-up, not registered; round-1 runs, one program per model per cell, 10 cells)

1. **Codex vs Sonnet: no hub difference.** In the median program, 37% of internal calls go into the top 3 functions for Codex and 36% for Sonnet. Codex is higher in 7 of 10 cells (5 of 8 outside COBOL). Sonnet's second round gives 6 of 10. Both COBOL cells favour Codex (33% vs 24%, 50% vs 26%), so the COBOL hub pattern shows up here too, but it does not hold outside COBOL.
2. **Codex vs Haiku: Codex has more hubs, but only because its programs are smaller.** Medians are 37% vs 22%, and Codex is higher in 9 of 10 cells with 1 tie (two-sided sign test p = 0.004, exploratory). Haiku's second round gives 10 of 10. Haiku writes more functions in all 10 cells (median 39 vs 18). With random wiring of the same size, Codex's top-3 share is also higher in 10 of 10. Above that size baseline, Codex is higher in only 6 of 10 (K2: 4 of 10).
3. **Codex has more calls per function than Sonnet: 1.73 vs 1.35, higher in 8 of 10 cells (p = 0.11).** Sonnet's second round gives 7 of 10. This matches the reversed S1. **Haiku's call chains are longer:** median depth 7.5 vs 6 for Codex, longer in 7 of 7 untied cells (p = 0.016). Its second round is longer in 8 of 9. This is also partly size: Haiku has more functions.
- Control: hub share is a stable habit of each model, probably because function count is. A model's two rounds differ by a median of 3 to 5 points; different models in the same cell differ by 11 to 13. Rounds are closer than models in 8 of 10 cells for Sonnet and 9 of 10 for Haiku. After the size baseline is removed, this drops to 5 and 6 of 10.
- Null results: in-degree Gini, never-called share and outside-call share show no consistent model difference. The outside-call share varies more between a model's two rounds than between models.

Caveats: exploratory, with 10 cells and one run per cell. I made 66 cell-count comparisons (11 measures by 6 model pairs), so expect a few 8-of-10 splits by chance. Codex has only one round: the replication rows reuse the same Codex programs. Edges come from matching called names to the program's own functions. A name shared by several functions links to all of them, and recursion is dropped. Dispatch through traits, interfaces and pointers, and COBOL GO TO/THRU fall-through, are invisible. The scanner's own stricter resolved graph gives the same directions (X > K 10/0, X vs S 6/3). Scans: chess-c-codex-r1, chess-rust-codex-r1 and chess-rust-codex-r2 use the formatter-wrapped rescans, because their D16 dirs hold no functions (as in registered S1). sat-c-codex-r1 uses its D16 rescan, which reports loc = 1 per function. No run was excluded. The sat-python Sonnet programs have only 4 functions, so their hub share is trivially 100%.
